## Civil times that are not one instant

A wall-clock time is not always a moment. The resolution policy is to take
the earliest candidate in every ambiguous case, because for a deadline the
earlier reading is the safe one.

| zone | civil time | ambiguity | candidates | chosen |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| America/New_York | 2026-11-01 01:30 | OVERLAP | 2 | 2026-11-01T05:30:00Z |
| America/New_York | 2026-03-08 02:30 | GAP | 0 | 2026-03-08T07:00:00Z |
| America/New_York | 2026-11-01 17:00 | UNIQUE | 1 | 2026-11-01T22:00:00Z |
| Australia/Lord_Howe | 2026-10-04 02:15 | GAP | 0 | 2026-10-03T15:30:00Z |

Lord Howe Island is in the table because its daylight shift is thirty
minutes, not sixty. Code that normalises an ambiguity by adding or
subtracting an hour is wrong there, and wrong without saying so.
